I used `look: "toon3d"` because the hard cel bands and black outlines suited a colorful toy robot and made the wooden blocks stay readable during the fast topple; `lit3d` would have felt more naturalistic than this subject needed. The shared `moveTo`/`spin3d`/`squashTo` vocabulary was excellent for choreography, and real lighting, cast shadows, per-face colors, and the automatic outline made a mesh-only scene feel finished without material or camera setup.

The main friction was that the 3D renderer does not inherit group transforms or support camera moves, depth-position animation, particles, sound, or 2D overlays, so a multipart character has to be posed piece by piece and even a head turn requires coordinating every facial mesh. I also wished `rotateTo` and `spin3d` shared clearer conflict diagnostics because both ultimately write the mesh's Z rotation. A simple transform hierarchy for mesh parts, explicit scene lighting controls, and 3D audio support would make longer shorts substantially easier while preserving the pleasantly small API.
